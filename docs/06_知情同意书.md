> 由文献调研 + 规范核对后起草。**正文为英文（直接给被试看），批注为中文（给我们看）。**
> 待填项见 §3 —— 共 **23 处**，按「谁能回答」分组。

## 0. ⚠️ 最重要的一条发现：IEEE VIS 2026 改规定了

**IEEE VIS 2026 的投稿指南新增了一节** *"Expectations for Reporting Research with Human Participants"*，援引 IEEE PSPB Operations Manual（该手册管辖 TVCG），**要求**：

> *"Authors of articles reporting on research involving human subjects … shall include a statement in the article that the research was performed under the oversight of an institutional review board or equivalent local/regional body, **including the official name of the IRB/ethics committee**, or include an explanation as to why such a review was not conducted. For research involving human subjects, authors shall also **report that consent from the human subjects in the research was obtained** or explain why consent was not obtained."*

并给出了可直接套用的范例措辞：

> *"Our experiment was approved by our university's IRB (No. 12345678). […] At the start of the experiment, we obtained informed consent from all participants, who filled in and signed a consent form (which we share in our additional materials)."*

### 合规现状极差

**Besançon & Isenberg, TVCG 2026**（*"More Than 63% of IEEE VIS Research Liable to be Retracted?!"*）筛查了 2024–2025 年的 **255 篇 IEEE VIS 论文**：

| | 篇数 | 占比 |
|---|---|---|
| 涉及人类被试 | 189 | 74.1% |
| **同时报告了伦理批号 + 同意书** | **6** | **3.2%** |
| 至少报告了伦理批准与同意 | 26 | 13.8% |

**含义**：
1. **我们必须准备一套完整的伦理材料**（批号 + 同意书），并在论文里写明
2. **这是加分项** —— 做到就超过 96.8% 的同类论文
3. **同意书建议作为 supplementary material 提交**（VIS 2026 的示例措辞就是这么写的）

### 顺带修正一处文献错误

常被引用的 **"GLE questionnaire" 这个名字不存在**。图素养的标准量表是 **Galesic & Garcia-Retamero (2011)**，缩写 **GGR**，13 题。我们如果只取 5–8 题，论文里应当称其为「study-specific subset」，**不要**声称用了完整量表。

### Informed Consent Form

**Study title:** How Graph Layout Affects the Readability of a Node's Neighbourhood

**Principal investigator:** [Full name], [Department], [University]
**Supervisor:** [Full name], [Department]
**Ethics approval:** [IRB/REB name], protocol no. [XXXX-XXXX]
**Version:** [1.0], [date]

**Please read this page before you decide whether to take part. Ask us anything that is unclear — now or at any time during the session.**

#### Key information

- We are studying how different ways of drawing network graphs affect how easily people can read a node's connections.
- You will complete one session of about **45–50 minutes** at [location].
- You will look at graphs and answer questions about them, and complete short questionnaires.
- The main discomfort is **tiredness or boredom**. You can take breaks and you can stop at any time.
- You will be paid **$10**, even if you stop early.
- We record your answers, whether they were correct, your response times, and your questionnaire answers. We do **not** record your name, email address, or IP address.
- Taking part is **voluntary**. Saying no has no consequences for you.

#### 1. Why are we doing this study?

Graphs made of dots (nodes) joined by lines (links) are used to show networks — friendship networks, computer networks, and many others. We are comparing several ways of laying out these graphs. We want to know which layouts make it easiest to see which nodes are connected to a particular node. The results will help designers choose better layouts.

We are **not** studying you. We are studying the layouts.

#### 2. Who can take part?

Anyone aged **18 or over** with normal or corrected-to-normal vision (glasses or contact lenses are fine).

The task highlights nodes in **blue**. If you have difficulty telling colours apart, please tell the researcher before we start.

#### 3. What will you do?

One session, about 45–50 minutes:

1. **Background questions** — your age, gender, field of study, and how familiar you are with graphs. (2 min)
2. **A short quiz** — 5–8 multiple-choice questions about reading charts and graphs. This tells us how much experience people bring to the task. **It is not a test of you and you cannot fail it.** (3 min)
3. **Instructions and 4 practice trials** — you can ask questions during this part. (5 min)
4. **48 trials.** In each trial you see one graph:
   - **Task A (24 trials):** one node is highlighted in blue. Click **every** node directly connected to it, then click **Submit**.
   - **Task B (24 trials):** two nodes are highlighted in blue. Click the one you think is **connected to more nodes**.

   There is **no time limit** on any trial, and no penalty for being slow.
5. **Breaks.** After every 12 trials the screen forces a **30-second break**. You may also pause between trials whenever you want, and you may leave the room with the researcher's knowledge at any point.
6. **Short questionnaires** — a few questions about how mentally demanding the task felt, and how readable you found the graphs. (5 min)

#### 4. Where the graphs come from

The graphs are based on **real computer-network data** — records of how internet domains and subdomains relate to one another. **The data have been fully anonymised before we use them:** every real name has been replaced with a number, so the graphs contain no identifying or personal information about any person or organisation. The original dataset is not published, and no part of it will be released with our results — we will only ever publish derived graph layouts or aggregate findings.

> **[TRIM — optional sentence if you want to be more explicit:]** You are not being asked to assess any real organisation, and the graphs are not connected to any current security incident.

#### 5. Some graphs appear more than once

You will see 48 graphs in total, and **some graphs appear more than once** — but you will be asked a **different question** each time. This is a normal part of the study design, not a mistake, and it is not a sign that something went wrong.

> **[TRIM — optional:]** If you notice a graph repeating, please just answer the question as it is asked.

#### 6. Risks and discomfort

This is a **minimal-risk** study. The risks are the same as those you face in everyday life. The discomforts we expect are:

- **Tired eyes** or **mental fatigue** from looking at graphs for an extended period
- **Boredom**, because the trials are repetitive
- **Mild frustration** if a graph is hard to read

To reduce these, we give you frequent breaks, there are no time limits, and there is no penalty for wrong answers. You may stop at any time.

You may also find that seeing many similar graphs is monotonous. If you would rather stop, just say so — you will still be paid in full.

#### 7. Benefits

There is **no direct benefit to you**. You will get a first-hand look at how a visualization experiment is run, and you will be contributing to research that helps people design more readable network graphs. We can share a summary of the results with you if you would like — ask the researcher.

#### 8. Payment

You will receive **$10** at the end of the session. This is paid **even if you stop early or skip questions**, and it does not depend on your performance.

#### 9. What we record

For each trial we record:

- which nodes you clicked
- whether your answer was correct
- how long you took to answer
- the number of times you switched away from the study window during the session

We also record your questionnaire answers, your background answers (age, gender, field of study, graph familiarity), and basic technical information from your browser (browser type and version, screen resolution).

We do **not** record your name, your email address, or your IP address.

> **[TRIM — drop this if your IRB objects to the wording:]** The window-switching count is used only to check data quality. It is not used to judge you, and it does not affect your payment.

#### 10. Confidentiality, storage, and how long we keep your data

- Your data are stored under a **participant code** (for example, `P017`), not under your name.
- The list linking your code to your name (used only for the payment record) is stored **separately** from your study data, in a [locked cabinet / password-protected file] accessible only to [PI name].
- Electronic data are stored on [encrypted, access-controlled university storage].
- Only the research team ([names/roles]) can see the data.
- **We keep the data for [X] years after the study ends and then securely destroy it.** Signed consent forms are kept separately for [X] years as required by [institution] for audit purposes.
- Once your data have been de-identified, **we may not be able to delete it on request**, because we will no longer be able to tell which data are yours.

#### 11. How we report results

We report **averages and summaries across all participants**, never individual responses. We may also publish de-identified data (participant code, clicks, times, questionnaire answers — with no names or identifying details) in a public research repository so others can check our analysis. **You may say no to this without affecting anything else.**

Taking part is **your choice**. If you decide not to take part, or if you stop partway through, there is **no penalty of any kind** and you do not lose any benefits you would otherwise have. Your decision will not affect your grades, your standing at [University], or your relationship with the researchers.

**If you stop:** you will still be paid in full. You may ask us to destroy your data at that point, and we will. After the session ends, you may [email [address]] within **[X days]** to withdraw your data. After that date the data will be de-identified and we will not be able to identify yours.

### 提交版（匿名）

> **Ethics and informed consent.** This study was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board of our institution (approval number withheld for anonymity). All procedures complied with the institution's human-subjects research policies. All participants provided informed consent before any data were collected. ### 终版（camera-ready）

> **Ethics and informed consent.** This study was reviewed and approved by [IRB NAME] (protocol no. [NUMBER]). All participants provided informed consent prior to data collection. Participants in the laboratory condition signed a written consent form; participants recruited through Prolific read the same information and provided consent via an explicit checkbox, timestamped and stored with their Prolific ID. ## 5. 两个判断题的结论

### ① 要不要披露图来自网络安全资产数据？—— **要**

不是法条要求，是判断。理由：

1. **Menlo Report 的核心立场**：保护范围延伸到**数据所代表的人**，关注点从「有没有人类被试」转向「有没有伤害人的可能」。这条推理适用于**刺激材料的来源**
2. **知情同意要求被试知道自己在做什么**。以为在看合成玩具图和知道在看真实基础设施，是两种不同的认知
3. **预判疑虑**：知道是真实网络数据的被试可能担心「我点的位置会不会泄露网络信息」—— 虽然不成立（图已匿名、我们不发布原数据），但**预先说明比事后发现好**
4. **留后路**：现在披露了，以后要发布刺激图不用回头改同意书
5. **安全社区惯例**：USENIX Security 的道德指南要求记录对「用户与旁观者」的负面影响

**长度控制**：2–3 句，**放在「你要做什么」之后**（不要拖慢关键信息）。

⚠️ **前提**：**先问资产数据的所有方**，连「real computer-network data」这句是否允许。若不允许，退路措辞：

> *"The graphs are derived from anonymised records of how internet domains relate to one another. They contain no personal information."*

### ② 要不要披露「有些图会重复出现」？—— **要**

1. **被试会注意到**。48 个试次、24 张图，重复是显眼的。**不解释的重复会被当成 bug** —— 以为程序坏了的被试会变慢、反复怀疑、放弃投入。这是**数据质量问题**，不只是礼貌问题
2. **不泄露假设**。「有些图会重复，但每次问题不同」对几乎任何被试内设计都成立，透露不了布局条件
3. **诚实**。隐瞒是小欺骗，按 Common Rule 和 Menlo Report 都需要论证并事后 debrief —— 为这点事不值得
4. **兜底**：若不披露，就必须在 debrief 里披露并提供撤回选项 —— 比一句话麻烦得多

⚠️ **一个注意**：**不要**告诉被试**哪些**图重复、**重复几次** —— 那会让他们数数、预判，污染反应时。本稿的措辞回避了这一点。
